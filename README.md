# Annotation: Mathematics as a Precise Instance of a General Human Practice

**Author:** Carolina Johnson (CJ)  
**Date:** October 2026  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23624551
**ORCID:** https://orcid.org/0009-0002-8819-3347

## Overview

This paper examines how humans record observed regularities in a form that can be preserved, communicated, and extended beyond the original observation. Across the symbolic systems examined, a recurring sequence appears: a regularity is observed, a conventional sign is fixed for it, the sign is compressed and transmitted, and eventually the sign can be mistaken for the thing it records. The paper traces that sequence through the historical record of counting and numeral notation, then compares it with spoken language and measurement. Mathematics is treated as a precise instance of this general practice rather than as a special case.

## Core Result

**Counting is correspondence by definition.**

The correspondence is established first, and the numerical sequence is the order along which that correspondence runs. The sequence is unbounded, and its unboundedness is a property of the rule that extends the notation, not of the flock being counted.

Numeral systems are distinct representational encodings of the same invariant quantity rather than competing truth-claims. The effectiveness of mathematics in describing the physical world is the expected consequence of a notation developed by observing the thing it describes. The failure mode of the practice is representation drift, the treatment of the annotation as the thing annotated, which the paper separates into encoding failure and scope failure.

Full derivations and worked examples are provided in the paper, including the number 2026 in four notations, the non-terminating binary expansion of one tenth, the Mars Climate Orbiter unit mismatch, and the angle sum of a spherical triangle.
